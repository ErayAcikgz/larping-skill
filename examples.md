# Larp Examples

All situations below are fictional and broadly applicable. Use them as patterns for evidence-based professional writing, not as claims to copy.

## 1. CV Bullet: Organizing Repeated Work

**Evidence provided**

> I made a spreadsheet for volunteer shifts and sent an update each week.

**Rewrite**

> Created and maintained a spreadsheet-based volunteer shift tracker, providing weekly updates to support scheduling coordination.

**Why it is safe**

The rewrite retains the stated spreadsheet and update work. It does not imply ownership of the full volunteer program or claim time savings that were not provided.

## 2. CV Bullet: Testing a Digital Form

**Evidence provided**

> I helped test a new online booking form and reported errors.

**Rewrite**

> Supported testing of a new online booking form by documenting observed errors for follow-up.

**Why it is safe**

The wording makes the testing contribution clear while preserving that it was collaborative work. It does not claim that the person built, released, or fixed the form.

## 3. Portfolio Narrative: Creating User Documentation

**Evidence provided**

> I wrote a short guide for new staff about using the check-in system.

**Rewrite**

> Produced a concise onboarding guide that documented the check-in workflow for new staff.

**Why it is safe**

The source supports creating documentation about the workflow. It does not claim that the guide changed training outcomes or that the person owned the underlying system.

## 4. Professional Profile: Data Preparation

**Evidence provided**

> I cleaned survey responses in a spreadsheet and made a chart for a class project.

**Rewrite**

> Prepared survey-response data in a spreadsheet and created a summary chart for a class project.

**Why it is safe**

The rewrite identifies the actual data-preparation and presentation work. It does not imply formal research findings, advanced analytics, or external publication.

## 5. Cover Letter: Customer-Facing Experience

**Evidence provided**

> I answered customer questions at a store and helped when products were not available.

**Rewrite**

> Built practical customer-service experience by responding to product questions and helping customers identify available alternatives.

**Why it is safe**

The text reflects the stated interactions and keeps the outcome narrow. It does not add customer-satisfaction metrics, sales impact, or supervisory responsibility.

## 6. Thin Evidence: Tool Exposure

**Evidence provided**

> I used Canva for social media posts.

**Cautious draft**

> Created social media graphics using Canva.

**Question**

> Did you create the content plan, write copy, review performance, or only produce visuals? One confirmed responsibility would support a more specific version.

**Why it is safe**

The draft claims only use of the named tool and the stated output. The question separates design work from strategy, copywriting, and performance analysis.

## 7. Unsupported Claim Rejected

**Evidence provided**

> I attended planning meetings for a community event.

**Claim to avoid**

> Led the planning and delivery of a community event.

**Safe alternative**

> Participated in planning meetings for a community event and gained exposure to event coordination discussions.

**Why it is safe**

Meeting attendance supports participation and exposure. It does not establish planning ownership, delivery responsibility, or leadership.

## 8. Completed Training

**Evidence provided**

> I completed workplace safety training.

**Rewrite**

> Completed workplace safety training covering the procedures required in the role.

**Why it is safe**

The wording preserves the completion of training. It does not claim certification, compliance authority, or responsibility for safety management.
